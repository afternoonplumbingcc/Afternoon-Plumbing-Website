Who fixes what

Utility vs house side: know who owns the pipe before you call

The utility owns the meter and everything upstream

Your gas utility, BGE, Washington Gas, or Columbia Gas, owns the street main, the service line to the home, and the meter itself. A smell at or strongest near the meter means a utility-side problem: shut off what you can from outside, move away, and call that utility's emergency dispatch. The crew comes out free of charge, around the clock, to make it safe.

We own the house side

Everything downstream of the meter or the propane tank is the homeowner's to repair: the regulator, the piping that runs to your appliances, CSST, and the connectors at each appliance. If the utility clears the scene and points to the house side, Afternoon Plumbing finds the leak, repairs it, and leak-tests the whole run before relighting anything.

Propane is different

On a propane home, your propane supplier owns everything up to and including the tank's first-stage regulator, not the gas utility. The supplier's number is printed on the tank or on your billing statement, and that is the emergency call. Once the supplier makes the tank side safe, the house-side piping, regulators, and connectors are ours to repair and leak-test.

When both companies come out

On many leak calls, the utility crew arrives first, stops the leak from their side or shuts the gas off, and leaves the home safe. We then get their all-clear, on the phone or on the work order, and start the house-side repair. We coordinate around their timeline so the job picks up exactly where the utility left off.

While you wait

What to do while you wait for the gas utility

You called the utility and the crew is on the way. From wherever you and your family are standing, here is the short checklist.

  • Stay out. Do not re-enter the home until the utility crew tests it and says it is safe
  • Do not start your car if it is parked close to the home
  • If the smell is strong outside too, or coming from the meter, keep neighbors and passersby away from the area
  • Let the utility crew test with their own instruments; they will tell you what is safe and what is not
  • Keep an eye on pets and family, and stay a safe distance from the building the whole time

Common questions

Gas leak FAQ

Should I call 911 or the gas company first?

Get outside first. From a safe distance, call 911 if the smell is strong, if you hear a hissing sound, or if anyone feels dizzy or sick. Otherwise call your gas utility emergency line: BGE 1-800-685-0123, Washington Gas 1-844-927-4427, or Columbia Gas 1-888-460-4332. The utility emergency crew is free and responds around the clock.

Can I fix a small gas leak myself?

No. Never tighten fittings, seal anything, or test a suspected leak yourself. Gas leaks are found with combustible-gas instruments and a leak-detection solution, and the repair has to be leak-tested before the gas is turned back on. Leave the home first, let the utility make it safe, then call a licensed plumber for the house-side repair.

What does the gas company do vs a plumber?

The gas company owns the main and the meter, so the utility emergency dispatch responds first and makes everything on its side safe. That visit is free. If the leak turns out to be downstream of the meter or tank, on the house side, the repair belongs to a licensed plumber like Afternoon Plumbing, and we quote it upfront before any work starts.

How do plumbers find a hidden gas leak?

A plumber isolates the house piping and pressure-tests it, then checks every joint and fitting with a leak-detection solution or an electronic gas detector. Once the leaking section is found, we repair or replace it and re-pressurize and re-test before relighting any appliance.

Is a small gas smell normal near a meter?

No. An occasional whiff right at an exterior meter vent after heavy rain or a pressure change deserves a call to the utility, but any smell inside the home is an emergency and you should leave and call from outside. When in doubt, treat it as real and let the utility decide with their instruments.

Who pays for the gas company visit?

Nothing. The gas utility emergency response is free to you; the utility handles its own lines and equipment. The house-side repair after the meter or tank is the homeowner's, and we quote it upfront before we start.
