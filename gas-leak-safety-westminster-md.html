Who fixes what

Utility vs house side: know who owns the pipe before you call

The utility owns the meter and everything upstream

Your gas utility, BGE, Washington Gas, or Columbia Gas, owns the street main, the service line to the home, and the meter itself. A smell at or strongest near the meter means a utility-side problem: move a safe distance away and call that utility's emergency dispatch. Do not operate the meter or any valves yourself mid-emergency. The crew comes out free of charge, around the clock, to make it safe.

We work on the homeowner's house side

Everything downstream of the meter or the propane tank belongs to the homeowner to repair: the regulator, the piping that runs to your appliances, CSST, and the connectors at each appliance. If the utility comes out, clears the scene, and confirms the leak is not in their equipment, that is when we come out - we find the leak, repair it, and leak-test the whole run before relighting anything.

Propane is different

On a propane home, your propane supplier owns everything up to and including the tank's first-stage regulator, not the gas utility. The supplier's number is printed on the tank or on your billing statement, and that is the emergency call. Once the supplier makes the tank side safe, the house-side piping, regulators, and connectors are ours to repair and leak-test.

When both companies come out

The usual flow: the utility crew comes out first, makes their side safe - sometimes shutting the gas off entirely - and checks their meter and line. If a leak is still present after the utility's visit and it turns out the leak is not in their equipment, it is house-side, and that is when we come out. We repair it, leak-test, and get the gas back on. One call to us gets that scheduled around their visit so you are not without hot water longer than needed.

Know the difference

Is it a gas leak or carbon monoxide?

Natural gas and propane

Utility companies add a sulfur or rotten-egg odor on purpose so leaks are easy to notice. If you smell that odor anywhere in or near the home, treat it as a leak and follow the emergency steps at the top of this page.

Carbon monoxide

Carbon monoxide has no smell at all. Headache, dizziness, nausea, or drowsiness that eases when you leave the house - especially around a running furnace, generator, or vehicle near the home - points to CO, not a gas leak. Get everyone into fresh air and call 911, then let the utility test the home.

While you wait

What to do while you wait for the gas utility

You called the utility and the crew is on the way. From wherever you and your family are standing, here is the short checklist.

  • Stay out. Do not re-enter the home until the utility crew tests it and says it is safe
  • Do not start your car if it is parked close to the home
  • If the smell is strong outside too, or coming from the meter, keep neighbors and passersby away from the area
  • Let the utility crew test with their own instruments; they will tell you what is safe and what is not
  • Keep an eye on pets and family, and stay a safe distance from the building the whole time

Common questions

Gas leak FAQ

Should I call 911 or the gas company first?

Get outside first. From a safe distance, call 911 and your gas utility emergency line - any gas smell indoors is a 911 call, and you should not wait to see whether symptoms grow stronger. Tap-to-dial numbers: BGE 1-877-778-7798 (serves Westminster and most of Carroll County), Washington Gas 1-844-927-4427, or Columbia Gas 1-888-460-4332 (Western Maryland). The utility emergency crew is free and responds around the clock.

Can I fix a small gas leak myself?

No. Never tighten fittings, seal anything, or test a suspected leak yourself. Gas leaks are found with combustible-gas instruments and a leak-detection solution, and the repair has to be leak-tested before the gas is turned back on. Leave the home first, let the utility make it safe, then call a licensed plumber for the house-side repair.

What does the gas company do vs a plumber?

The gas company owns the main and the meter, so the utility emergency dispatch responds first and makes everything on its side safe. That visit is free. If the leak turns out to be downstream of the meter or tank, on the house side, the repair belongs to a licensed plumber like Afternoon Plumbing, and we quote it upfront before any work starts.

How do plumbers find a hidden gas leak?

A plumber isolates the house piping and pressure-tests it, then traces every joint and fitting with a combustible gas leak detector and leak-detection solution. Once the leaking section is found, we repair or replace it and re-pressurize and re-test before relighting any appliance.

Is a small gas smell normal near a meter?

No. An occasional whiff right at an exterior meter vent after heavy rain or a pressure change deserves a call to the utility, but any smell inside the home is an emergency and you should leave and call from outside. When in doubt, treat it as real and let the utility decide with their instruments.

Who pays for the gas company visit?

Nothing. The gas utility emergency response is free to you; the utility handles its own lines and equipment. The house-side repair after the meter or tank is the homeowner's, and we quote it upfront before we start.
