What we replace

Signs your regulator needs to be replaced

  • Sulfur or rotten-egg smell near the regulator or gas line
  • Visible corrosion, pitting, or green patina on the regulator body
  • Frost or ice forming on the regulator in cold weather
  • Soot, scorch marks, or discoloration around the regulator vent
  • Appliances that flame yellow, pop, or go out unexpectedly
  • Hissing sounds or bubbles when a leak-detection solution is applied
  • Age: most regulators last 10-15 years before they start to fail
Propane vs natural gas

Two fuels, same job, slightly different setup

Propane regulators

On propane systems, the first-stage regulator is usually mounted on the exterior wall of the home or directly on the propane tank. It drops tank pressure down to a level the home's appliances can use. Corrosion is the most common failure mode, especially on older two-pound adjustable regulators that have been in place for over a decade.

Natural gas regulators

On natural gas, the utility owns the meter and the regulator attached to it. A secondary regulator - needed for high-pressure appliances or specific equipment - can live indoors or outside the house; we have replaced both. We replace house-side natural gas regulators wherever they sit.

Who's responsible for what

The gas utility or propane supplier owns everything up to and including the meter (or up to the outlet of the first-stage tank regulator). Everything downstream of that is the homeowner's responsibility. We work on the house side and coordinate with the utility if the issue turns out to be upstream.

When the utility needs to come first

If the leak is at the meter, on the utility side of the meter, or in the line between the meter and the house before our regulator, the utility needs to dispatch first.

What goes into the quote

What affects the cost of a regulator replacement?

The regulator type

A standard single-stage house-side regulator is a simpler swap than a two-pound or high-capacity unit. The type is set by your tank or meter setup, which we confirm on the estimate visit.

The condition of the connecting pipe

Years of weather can leave the surrounding pipe and fittings rusted or seized. Connectors in good shape make the swap straightforward; corroded ones add work we will spot and show you during the estimate.

Downstream fittings

If the leak reached the fittings or the flex connector, those need replacing too - the estimate covers the full path, not just the regulator body.

Common questions

Gas regulator FAQ

If I smell gas, what should I do first?

Leave the home, do not flip light switches or use anything that could spark, and call your gas utility or 911 from a safe distance. Once the utility confirms it is safe to re-enter, call Afternoon Plumbing to repair the house-side regulator or gas line.

What is a propane or natural gas regulator?

A regulator reduces high-pressure gas from the tank or utility meter down to the lower pressure your home appliances use. On propane systems, the regulator is usually mounted on the exterior wall of the home. On natural gas, it can be on the meter or at the appliance.

How do I know if my gas regulator is failing?

Common signs include a sulfur or rotten-egg smell near the regulator, visible corrosion on the regulator body, frost or ice on the regulator in cold weather, soot or discoloration around the vent, and appliances that flame yellow instead of blue. Any active leak should be treated as an emergency.

Can a plumber replace a gas regulator?

Yes. Licensed plumbers in Maryland routinely replace house-side propane or natural gas regulators, including the line between the meter or tank and the home. The utility owns everything up to and including the meter; everything downstream is the property owner's responsibility and is where we work.

How long does a regulator replacement take?

Most single regulator swaps are a short visit, including leak testing the new connection. We verify pressure, check for leaks at every joint, and confirm the appliance side is operating correctly before restoring service.

Will my gas be shut off during the replacement?

Yes, the gas is shut off at the tank or meter for the duration of the swap. Once the new regulator is installed and tested, we restore service and relight any affected appliances.

Do you handle propane or natural gas regulators?

Yes. We replace house-side regulators on propane tanks and natural gas meters across Westminster and Carroll County.
