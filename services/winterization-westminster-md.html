What we winterize

Six systems checked on one visit

Your home depends on water and gas systems that all react to cold weather. When water trapped in a pipe or fixture freezes it expands, and can split copper, CPVC, and even brass, leaving you with a repair that includes the drywall and flooring it soaked. Here is exactly what a winterization visit checks, and where each piece connects to the dedicated service page if it needs real work.

1. Outdoor hose bibs

Hose bibs and hose connections

We disconnect hoses, drain the lines, and recommend frost-free hose bib replacement where a standard bib is a repeat freeze risk.

2. Water heaters

Water heater cold season check

We check the pressure relief valve and its setting, confirm the drain valve works, check venting and flue clearance, look for freeze exposure in unheated areas such as basements, garages and crawl spaces, and check sediment and anode rod condition. See the water heater replacement page if the unit is due for more than a checkup.

3. Well systems

Well pumps and well piping

We verify the pitless or above ground pump and piping are not exposed to freezing, confirm the pressure tank and controls are in order, and check the well casing seal. For deeper problems, start with well pump repair.

4. Softeners and filtration

Water softeners and filtration

We check the drain line for freezing, confirm the brine line and air injector are not blocked with ice, and check brine tank salt levels going into the cold season. For new equipment, see water softener installation.

5. Treatment systems

Water treatment and acid neutralizer

We confirm the drain line runs clear and is protected from freezing, and that the media is in good shape going into winter. If the system is aging, see water treatment replacement.

6. Gas systems

Gas appliances and gas piping

We confirm the regulator and gas appliances are operating properly and safely for the season, consistent with our gas leak safety guidance and gas appliance hookups services.

Do it yourself

Homeowner steps you can take now

These are general guidance steps any homeowner can handle. If anything beyond this list needs attention, call us.

  1. Disconnect garden hoses and drain them before storing them for winter.
  2. Close the interior supply valves that feed outdoor faucets, then open the outside tap so water can drain out.
  3. Keep heat on in areas with vulnerable pipes, such as basements, crawl spaces, and garages.
  4. During hard freezes, open cabinet doors on exterior walls so indoor warmth reaches the pipes inside.
  5. Know where your main shutoff is and make sure it turns, before you need it in an emergency.
  6. Never use a torch or open flame to thaw a frozen pipe. Warm it gently with a hair dryer instead.

FAQ

Winterization questions

What is plumbing winterization?

Winterization is a seasonal preventive visit that gets your plumbing and gas systems ready for freezing weather. We check outdoor hose bibs, water heaters, well pumps and piping, water softeners and filtration, treatment systems, and gas appliances so small issues get fixed before they turn into freeze damage.

When should I winterize my home in Maryland?

The best time is early to mid fall, before the first hard freeze. In Carroll County that usually means getting the visit scheduled in October or early November while there is still time to replace parts comfortably.

Is winterization only for homes on well water?

No. Homes on public water still have hose bibs, water heaters, softeners, filtration, and gas appliances that need cold season checks. Well homes simply add the pump, pressure tank, and wellhead items to the same visit.

What is the risk if I skip winterization?

Frozen pipes can crack hose bibs, split supply lines, and leave you without water until repairs are done. A water heater or treatment system that fails in cold weather tends to be a worse emergency than the same problem caught during a fall visit.

Can I winterize my hose bibs myself?

Yes, the basic steps are simple: disconnect and drain garden hoses, close the interior supply valve, and leave the outside tap open so water can drain out. If the interior valve is stuck, missing, or the bib keeps freezing anyway, that is the part we handle.

What does a water heater winterization check include?

We confirm the pressure relief valve and its setting, check that the drain valve works, check venting and flue clearance, look for freeze exposure in unheated areas, and check sediment and anode rod condition.

How do I thaw a frozen pipe safely?

Shut off the main shutoff first so a cracked pipe cannot flood the house, then warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer or towels soaked in warm water. Never use a torch or any open flame. If the pipe has already split or you cannot reach it, call us at 443-789-2897.

How much does a winterization visit cost?

We give a free estimate and an upfront quote before any work starts, with no hidden fees. The exact quote depends on what your home has and what the inspection finds, so call or text us to talk through your setup.
