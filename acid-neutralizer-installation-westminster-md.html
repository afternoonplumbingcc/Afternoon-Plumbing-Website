Why pH correction matters

Low-pH well water slowly eats at copper plumbing and water-using appliances. A properly sized neutralizer stops that corrosion at the source.

Warning signs

Signs your Westminster well water may be acidic

  • Blue-green or green stains on drains, faucets, and fixtures
  • Pinhole leaks in copper pipe or corrosion at fitting connections
  • Metallic taste in drinking water, especially first thing in the morning
  • Frequent faucet, showerhead, or fixture replacement
  • Water heater connections or valves corroding faster than expected
  • Water test shows pH below 7.0, often in the 6.0-6.5 range

If you notice any of these, the next step is a free water test to confirm the actual pH and hardness before choosing equipment.

How it works

How acid neutralizers correct low pH

An acid neutralizer is a whole-house tank filled with calcite, a natural calcium carbonate media. As acidic water flows through the tank, the calcite slowly dissolves and adds alkalinity, raising pH toward the neutral 7.0-7.5 range.

A built-in backwash cycle flushes the bed on a timer to keep the media loose, clean, and free of channeling. For wells with very low pH, we may use a calcite and corosex blend. The blend and tank size are chosen from your water test results, not from a generic formula.

This is pH water treatment, not filtration. It changes the chemistry of the water so it stops attacking your plumbing from the inside out.

Ongoing care

Acid neutralizer maintenance

Acid neutralizers are simple, but they are not zero-maintenance. The calcite media is consumed as it neutralizes acidic water, so the tank needs periodic media top-ups. We handle refills at material cost for systems we install.

The backwash schedule is set during installation based on your water conditions and usage. If your pH, water use, or well output changes, we can adjust the cycle. We also check valves, fittings, and the bypass during service visits to catch small issues before they turn into leaks.

FAQ

Acid neutralizer questions

What is acidic water and why does it matter?

Acidic water has a pH below 7.0. In Westminster and surrounding Carroll County, many wells run in the 6.0-6.5 range. Over time, low-pH water can corrode copper plumbing, cause pinhole leaks, stain fixtures with blue-green marks, and shorten the life of water heaters and appliances.

What causes blue-green stains in sinks and tubs?

Blue-green or green staining is usually copper corrosion dissolved into the water by low pH. It shows up around drains, faucets, and toilet bowls where the acidic water contacts copper plumbing or brass fittings. The fix is to raise pH with an acid neutralizer, not just scrub the stain away.

How does an acid neutralizer work?

An acid neutralizer tank holds calcite media, a natural mineral that slowly dissolves into the water as it passes through. The dissolved calcite raises pH toward neutral. A periodic backwash cycle keeps the media bed clean and prevents channeling. Very low pH wells may use a calcite and corosex blend sized to your test results.

Acid neutralizer or water softener: do I need both?

They treat different things. A neutralizer corrects acidity. A water softener addresses hardness minerals like calcium and magnesium. Because neutralizers add a small amount of hardness, many Carroll County homes pair the two systems. We test both pH and hardness before recommending one, the other, or both.

How much maintenance does an acid neutralizer need?

The calcite media is consumed as it neutralizes acidic water, so it needs top-ups periodically. We service what we install and refill media at material cost. The backwash schedule is set during install based on your water usage and conditions.

Can you test my water's pH?

Yes. We test pH on site during a free estimate visit and confirm whether an acid neutralizer is the right fix. We can also test hardness so the full water treatment plan fits your well. See more in our water treatment FAQ.
