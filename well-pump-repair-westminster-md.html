Diagnosis

Why your well stopped: common causes

  • Tripped breaker or blown fuse Power loss is the fastest fix when it is just a breaker.
  • Failed pressure switch No click, burned contacts, or a switch stuck open stops the pump.
  • Waterlogged pressure tank A failed bladder makes the pump cycle rapidly and wears it out.
  • Worn or failed pump Age, sediment, and overheating eventually end a pump's life.
  • Broken pipe or fitting A leak between the well and house can drop pressure fast.

We sort symptoms from causes.

A well pump problem is not always the pump. Sometimes the issue is the switch, tank, electrical supply, or a leak in the line. We test the system in the right order so you do not pay for a pump you did not need.

Equipment

Pump types explained

Submersible well pumps

These sit deep inside the well and push water up. They are common in newer and deeper wells across Carroll County. Submersibles are generally quiet, efficient, and long-lasting.

Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water from shallow wells. You will usually hear them in a basement or utility room. Jet pumps cost less up front but can struggle with deeper water.

Booster and constant pressure systems

If your shower pressure drops when another faucet runs, a booster pump or constant pressure system may help. These keep pressure steady across the house instead of the old 40-60 on-off cycle.

Not every home needs one. We compare the fix against the existing pump and tank first so you get an honest recommendation.

Repair vs replace

How we decide whether to repair or replace

We look at three things on every well service call:

  • Gauge behavior Does pressure build, hold, or drop immediately?
  • Cycling pattern Rapid on-off cycles point to a tank or switch issue.
  • Amp draw and wiring Electrical testing tells us if the pump is working hard or failing.

Repair makes sense when the pump is relatively new and the failure is a switch, tank, or minor electrical issue. Replacement is the better call when the pump is at the end of life, repeatedly failing, or pulling high amps.

FAQ

Well pump questions

I have no water, what should I check first?

Make sure the whole house is out, not just one fixture. Then check the circuit breaker that powers the pump, the pressure switch for clicks or burned contacts, and the pressure tank gauge. If water does not return, call 443-789-2897.

Is it the pump or the pressure tank?

Short cycling, a waterlogged tank, or a gauge that drops fast usually point to the pressure tank or switch. If the pump runs but pressure never builds, the pump, wiring, or well itself may be the issue. We test on site before recommending a fix.

How long do well pumps last?

Most well pumps last 8-15 years depending on water quality, usage, pump type, and depth. A constant on-and-off cycle or sediment can shorten life. We inspect the whole system so you know whether age is the real factor.

Can you replace a well pump the same day?

Same-day replacement is possible when the right pump is available and the well head is accessible. We always diagnose first, then quote the repair or replacement with no pressure.

Submersible vs jet pump, which do I have?

Submersible pumps sit deep inside the well and are common in newer and deeper wells. Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water from a shallow well. During the visit we identify the pump type and explain what that means for repair options.

What does well pump replacement cost?

The total depends on pump type, well depth, wiring condition, and whether the pressure tank, switch, or check valve also need attention. We quote after diagnosing, free.

Would a constant pressure system help my low pressure?

A constant pressure or booster system can help homes where pressure drops when multiple fixtures run. It is not always necessary. We compare the cost and benefit against fixing the existing pump and tank first.
